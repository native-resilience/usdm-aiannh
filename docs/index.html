What this is

The US Drought Monitor (USDM) is the weekly national drought map. This dashboard reports it for every American Indian, Alaska Native, and Native Hawaiian area in the US Census: the share of each area in each drought class this week, and every week since January 2000.

Where Census splits an area into a reservation and off-reservation trust land, the two are reported separately — their drought conditions can differ.

Reading the numbers

  • D0 abnormally dry · D1 moderate · D2 severe · D3 extreme · D4 exceptional drought.
  • Area in drought is the share of land in D1 or worse, as on drought.gov.
  • The history chart shows the share at or above each class (D0–D4, D1–D4, …), drawn over one another, so the top of each color is that cumulative share.
  • The map colors each area by the most severe class covering any part of it.
  • Percentages are of the whole Census boundary, including water, which the coastline-clipped USDM map counts as no drought.

Using the dashboard

  • Click an area, or find it by name in the header. Every area is reachable by keyboard.
  • The address bar always holds your current view; copy it to share.
  • Each area links to d3drought.org for the latest precipitation, evaporative demand, and drought index maps.
